# Alpha v21: constructive matrix and certified-algorithm foundations

Alpha v21 preserves all 1,776 independently checked Alpha-v20 theorem entries,
every historical release artifact, and the unchanged 432-theorem Stable
channel. It appends exactly 54 dependency-ordered original-kernel proofs:

| Constructive campaign | New theorems | Exact proved scope |
| --- | ---: | --- |

The resulting Alpha release has exactly 1,830 independently checked theorem
rows, 5,986 actual direct dependency edges, and 53 dependency layers. Stable
remains unchanged. Its exact Alpha-v20 parent is bound by all four historical
artifact digests and its immutable statement/evidence identities.

All 54 new rows are justified by one self-contained ordinary intuitionistic
proof bundle. Its exact full prerequisite cone contains 154 unchanged parent
theorems, the 54 new theorems, and one balanced, unenrolled synthetic
conjunction root. Its 209 graph nodes have 491 exact local dependency edges;
each ordinary dependency-curried proof body is independently accepted by the
original unchanged Heyting-arithmetic checker.

Named mathematical definitions are parsed, hygienically expanded conservative
first-order abbreviations; they are never new predicates, axioms, or proof
rules. No theorem uses double-negation elimination, an oracle, an admitted
lemma, an unchecked dependency, or an untrusted external computation.

The exact historical blueprint milestone T13 remains **open** despite full
arbitrary-dimensional signed multiplication: arbitrary-dimensional signed
determinants, rank, and lattice infrastructure have not been proved.

The exact G101 milestone remains **open**. The new Euclidean relation provides

that gcd. Its proved linear bound and genuine two-step halving lemma are not
the missing formal `BitLen` relation and global logarithmic-bound induction.

The exact G102 milestone also remains **open**. The checked first-order results
prove square-and-multiply transitions and the unique modular-power result;
the executable independently verifies finite coded runs satisfying the
desired bit-length bound. However, no checked object-language theorem yet

and the exact logarithmic bound.
