## Mathematical scope

These statements concern a prime modulus `p` and the actual canonical
coefficient prefixes specified in each theorem. They are not arbitrary-modulus
claims. Formal polynomial equivalence compares zero-extended coefficients,
not evaluation values or equality of the beta codes.

- Construct actual canonical common-length representatives, including empty
  inputs, leading-zero removal, and independent beta recodings. Addition and
  subtraction have genuine coefficient witnesses, formal uniqueness,
  cancellation, and associativity at independent original lengths.
- Every actual division execution supplies a proper-length product `Q*B` and
  the aligned identity `A=Q*B+R`, including the empty-quotient branch. The
  actual common right divisors of `(A,B)` and `(B,R)` coincide.
- From an actual Bézout representation `G=U*B+V*R`, construct `W=V*Q`,
  `T=U-W`, and the actual representation `G=V*A+T*B`. Multiplication order is
  preserved; polynomial commutativity is not assumed.
- A division remainder has retained length at most `d`, hence strictly less
  than divisor length `S d`. A nonzero constant divisor leaves an empty
  remainder. Zero receives no represented nonnegative degree.
- Construct a zero-or-monic right associate of every canonical polynomial,
  with actual quotient/product witnesses for both divisibility directions.
  Nonzero normalization uses genuine left-constant products and an inverted
  leading coefficient. Unit scaling is not falsely called formal equality.
